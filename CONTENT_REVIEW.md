# Content review — the 100-night calendar

The checked-in calendar schedules 100 unique dated cases from 13 November 2026 through 20 February 2027. Each builds eight connected evidence puzzles: 800 Daily puzzles. Three standalone Practice cases add 24 pages; the replayable tutorial adds two.

Cases vary names, timing, permits, cipher keys, words, routes, switch labels, relay parameters, settings and story titles across shared puzzle families. They are generated deterministically from published calendar records, not written by runtime AI. There are ten city settings, ten incident titles and four relay variants. This is a finite authored system, not a claim of 800 unrelated handcrafted mechanisms or endless fresh content.

Every puzzle requests typed facts with visible formats; some require multiple facts. All fields must match. The evidence explicitly defines permit start/end boundaries, fast/slow clocks and reporting delays, room adjacency versus direction, cipher operations and wrapping, one-way passages and no waiting, switch logic, and intermediate versus final relay values. The final six-field record draws on earlier pages, which can be opened with the chase paused.

Difficulty comes from combining rules, not missing facts or outside knowledge. Clues progress through permit constraints; two-step clock correction; room permutation; a cross-page alternating cipher and reversal; graph travel plus timestamp alibis; a five-switch Boolean interlock; and state toggles, reversed-digit equations, unknown-tray weight equations or a stateful counter. Page budgets grow from 65 to 150 seconds. The first tutorial lesson is untimed, penalty-free and spells out the input. The second demonstrates an actual chase.

The validator checks 100 consecutive dates, 800 Daily pages, unique evidence signatures, identifiers, valid field formats, one computed solution per clue and progression. Room solutions enumerate all 24 arrangements; interlocks enumerate all 32 switch states; reversed-digit locks enumerate all 900 three-digit numbers. Unit tests complete all 100 cases and independently parse clock prose, traverse the directed route graphs and recover unknown tray weights. Generated audits do not substitute for a human reading the prose. Automated checks cannot calibrate how hard a human finds a puzzle; broad player testing remains unrun.

Case files lock future Daily dates in the UI. Released cases and standalone cases remain playable in Practice. v4 separates old multiple-choice scores and saves; sound/motion preferences migrate. Only the new key is reset. Data and answers are client-side in this static single-player game; no competitive anti-cheat claim is made.
